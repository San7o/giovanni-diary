* Writing Notes

Here are my notes on writing, dialogues, exposition, narration, mood...

** Forms

   One of the first things you will have to choose before writing anything is
   its form. That is its physical shape, format or general structure of the
   text. Different forms give different prospectives over a fact / feeling so
   you should choose wisely.

   Some common forms are:

   - journal entry: either writing down the inner thoughts of a character
     during a moment (stream of conciousness) or afterwards.
   - letter (epistle): here the character is writing to somebody for some reason
   - essay
   - poem: when you want to transmit a feeling or a moment
   - short story
   - speech
** Structure

Every piece of writing needs to have some kind of structure to carry on the
reader. Some structures are more popular than others, here are a few useful
ones to keep in mind:

- Three-Acts: Setup (Act I), Confrontation / Development (Act II) and Resolution
  (Act III). To move the story forward, identify the inciting incident (the
  spark that starts the story) and the climax (the point of no return)

- Scene and Sequel: alternate these two phases for pacing. A scene is about
  action (goal -> conflict -> disaster). A sequel is the emotional aftermath
  (Reaction -> Dilemma -> Decision).

** Character Building

Characters are the center of the plot. They do not necessarily need to be human,
a character can be a tree or a house. To build a character you need to think
about their internal architecture.

- what they want: this is what the character thinks will make them happy. It
  drives the plot forward
- what they need: this is the emotional lesson or truth the character must learn
  to truly be fulfilled. Usually, they are blind to it at the start.

A story is truly born when a character's want directly forces them to confront
their need. The tension between these two is where the drama lives.

Keep in mind the distinction between:

- Characterization: the sum of all observable traits like age, accent,
  clothing style, job and habits.
- True Character: is is revealed only through choices made under pressure

The most important page for any character is the one where they are introduced.
Instead of describing a character, show them doing something that summarizes
their entire personality.

** Point of View

There are multiple point of view. This greatly influences the psychological
distance between the reader and the character.

- First Person
- Third person Limited
- Third Person Omniscient

** Style

Style really focuses at the sentence (or multiple sentences) level, choosing what
kind of grammar and vocabulary to use. Some tips are:

- Ladder of Abstractions: Alternate high abstract words (justice, emotion...)
  with low abstraction words (concrete image like a rusted car, a bloody lip...).
- Iceberg Theory: theory of omission, where the true subject is never explicitly
  stated

** Dialogues

There are many types of dialogues, depending on the situation, characters,
topic, etc...

There are many variables that influence the speech itself, like volume, pitch
and speed.

Tips:

- dialogues help you show, not tell
